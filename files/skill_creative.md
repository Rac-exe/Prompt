# SKILL FILE: CREATIVE

## WHEN TO USE THIS SKILL

Use this skill when the request involves:
- Writing stories, narratives, or fiction
- Creating blog posts, articles, or content
- Copywriting or marketing content
- Brainstorming ideas or concepts
- Character development or worldbuilding
- Poetry or creative writing
- Social media content
- Brand voice or messaging
- Creative problem-solving

**Keywords that trigger this skill:**
story, blog, article, content, creative, write, narrative, character, plot, copy, marketing, brand, social media, post, caption, slogan, campaign, brainstorm, idea

---

## OPTIMIZATION CHECKLIST

When optimizing creative prompts, ensure you've addressed:

✓ **Tone and Voice Defined?**
  - Formal, casual, playful, authoritative, empathetic?
  - First-person, third-person, second-person?
  - Brand voice if applicable

✓ **Audience Identified?**
  - Who will read/consume this?
  - What's their background, interests, pain points?
  - What language level (general public, experts, children)?

✓ **Purpose Clear?**
  - Inform, entertain, persuade, inspire, educate?
  - What action should the audience take?
  - What emotion should they feel?

✓ **Format Specified?**
  - Length (word count, paragraphs, pages)
  - Structure (sections, bullet points, narrative flow)
  - Medium (blog, email, social post, script, story)

✓ **Constraints and Requirements?**
  - Must include certain topics or themes
  - Must avoid certain topics
  - SEO keywords to incorporate
  - Brand guidelines to follow

✓ **Style and Inspiration?**
  - References to similar works
  - Mood or atmosphere desired
  - Stylistic techniques to use

## OPTIMIZATION TEMPLATE

Use this structure for creative prompts:

```
You are a [creative professional role] with expertise in [specific area].

CONTEXT:
[Background: purpose, audience, platform, brand if relevant]

TASK:
[Specific creative objective]

AUDIENCE:
- Demographics: [age, profession, interests]
- Knowledge Level: [novice/intermediate/expert in topic]
- Intent: [what they're looking for]
- Pain Points: [what problems they have]

REQUIREMENTS:
- Tone: [specific descriptors]
- Voice: [first/third person, formal/casual]
- Length: [specific word count or paragraphs]
- Format: [structure and organization]
- Must Include: [required elements]
- Must Avoid: [prohibited elements]
- Style: [descriptive qualities]

CREATIVE APPROACH:
[Suggested techniques, angles, or structures]

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- [Measurable quality indicator 1]
- [Measurable quality indicator 2]
- [Emotional or engagement goal]
```

## TECHNIQUE APPLICATION

**Primary Techniques for Creative Tasks:**

1. **Role Assignment**
   - Be specific about creative specialty
   - Example: "You are a senior copywriter specializing in SaaS product launches with 8 years at tech startups"
   - Not just: "You are a writer"

2. **Context Layering**
   - Audience context: who, why they care, what they need
   - Situational context: where will this appear, what's happening around it
   - Brand context: company voice, values, positioning
   - Emotional context: what feeling to evoke

3. **Output Specification**
   - Exact word count or range
   - Structural requirements (intro, 3 body sections, conclusion)
   - Tone descriptors (conversational but professional, empathetic yet authoritative)
   - Include examples of what you want: "Like this style: [example]"

4. **Few-Shot Framing**
   - Provide example styles or structures
   - "Write in the style of [publication/author]"
   - "Use the structure: Hook → Problem → Solution → Call to Action"

5. **Constraints**
   - Keywords to include (for SEO)
   - Topics to avoid (controversial subjects, competitors)
   - Brand voice guidelines (friendly, never salesy)
   - Length limits (280 characters for tweets)

## COMMON IMPROVEMENTS

### ❌ BAD: Vague Content Prompt
```
"Write a blog post about AI"
```

### ✅ GOOD: Optimized Content Prompt
```
You are a tech blogger with expertise in explaining AI to non-technical audiences.

CONTEXT:
Writing for a business blog read by SMB owners and managers who are curious about AI but intimidated by technical jargon. Goal is to demystify AI and show practical applications for small businesses.

TASK:
Write an engaging, accessible blog post that explains how small businesses can use AI tools without technical expertise.

AUDIENCE:
- Small business owners and managers
- Age 35-55, limited technical background
- Interested in efficiency and cost savings
- Skeptical of hype, want practical information

REQUIREMENTS:
- Tone: Conversational, encouraging, practical (not preachy or overly enthusiastic)
- Voice: Second-person ("you"), like talking to a friend
- Length: 1,200-1,500 words
- Format:
  - Attention-grabbing intro (hook + thesis)
  - 3-4 main sections with subheadings
  - Each section: explanation + practical example + specific tool recommendation
  - Conclusion with clear next steps
- Must Include:
  - 3-5 specific AI tools (ChatGPT, Copy.ai, etc.) with use cases
  - Real examples of how SMBs use each tool
  - Rough cost estimates (free tier or paid)
  - Links to getting started
- Must Avoid:
  - Technical jargon without explanation
  - Overhyped claims about AI
  - Generic advice ("just try it!")
  - Promoting specific brands too heavily
- SEO: Include keywords: "AI for small business", "business automation", "AI tools for entrepreneurs"

CREATIVE APPROACH:
- Start with a relatable problem (e.g., overwhelmed entrepreneur juggling tasks)
- Use storytelling: real small business examples throughout
- Break complex concepts into simple analogies
- End each section with "Try this:" actionable takeaway

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- A non-technical reader can understand and act on the advice
- Includes at least 3 specific, actionable tools they can try today
- Balances enthusiasm with realism
- Feels helpful, not salesy
- Average reading time: 5-6 minutes
```

### ❌ BAD: Unclear Story Prompt
```
"Write a short story about a detective"
```

### ✅ GOOD: Optimized Story Prompt
```
You are a crime fiction writer known for atmospheric noir detective stories.

TASK:
Write a short detective story with a twist ending.

SETTING:
- 1940s Los Angeles, noir atmosphere
- Rain-soaked streets, dim jazz clubs, shadowy alleys
- Post-war era, sense of disillusionment

CHARACTER:
- Detective: Jack Morrison, late 40s, cynical but moral
- World-weary private investigator, drinks too much coffee
- Has a code: never takes cases involving kids
- Haunted by an unsolved case from his past

PLOT REQUIREMENTS:
- Opens with Jack receiving a mysterious client visit
- Client seems nervous, hiding something
- Investigation leads to three suspects, each with motive
- Twist: The client is actually [leave room for creative choice]
- Must involve a betrayal or deception
- Resolution is bittersweet, not happy

STYLE:
- Tone: Dark, atmospheric, slightly melancholic
- Voice: Third-person limited (Jack's perspective)
- Prose: Lean, punchy sentences. Noir sensibility.
- Dialogue: Sharp, subtext-heavy. Characters don't say what they mean.
- Sensory details: Rain, cigarette smoke, neon signs, jazz music

STRUCTURE:
- Length: 2,000-2,500 words
- Three acts: Setup, Investigation, Revelation
- Hook in first paragraph
- Foreshadowing the twist without giving it away
- Satisfying but unexpected resolution

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- Reader feels the noir atmosphere immediately
- Jack is complex and sympathetic despite flaws
- Twist is surprising but makes sense in retrospect
- Prose is tight and evocative
- Ending feels inevitable but unexpected
```

### ❌ BAD: Generic Marketing Prompt
```
"Write a social media post about our new product"
```

### ✅ GOOD: Optimized Marketing Prompt
```
You are a social media copywriter specializing in B2B SaaS product launches.

CONTEXT:
Announcing the launch of "FlowTrack" - a project management tool for remote teams. Target audience is tech startup founders and project managers tired of juggling multiple tools. Platform is LinkedIn. This is the official launch announcement.

TASK:
Write a LinkedIn post announcing FlowTrack's public launch that drives sign-ups.

AUDIENCE:
- Startup founders, CTOs, product managers
- Managing remote teams of 5-50 people
- Currently frustrated with existing project tools (too complex or too simple)
- Active on LinkedIn, respond to authentic, value-first content

PRODUCT DETAILS:
- FlowTrack: Simple project management with built-in time tracking
- Key benefit: One tool instead of three (replaces Asana + Toggl + Slack for updates)
- Unique: Automated daily summaries, async-first design
- Launch offer: Free for teams under 10 people, $8/user/month after

REQUIREMENTS:
- Tone: Professional but not corporate. Conversational yet credible.
- Voice: First-person plural ("we"), like a founder talking to peers
- Length: 150-200 words (LinkedIn sweet spot)
- Format:
  - Hook (relatable problem in first sentence)
  - Brief intro (what we built and why)
  - 2-3 bullet points (key benefits, not features)
  - Call to action (link to free trial)
  - Hashtags: 3-5 relevant ones
- Must Include:
  - Specific pain point (tool overload)
  - Differentiation (what makes it different)
  - Launch incentive (free tier)
  - Clear CTA
- Must Avoid:
  - Overhyped language ("revolutionary", "game-changing")
  - Feature dumps
  - Generic promises ("boost productivity")
  - Pushy sales language

CREATIVE APPROACH:
- Open with a relatable frustration: "Anyone else have 12 browser tabs open just to update your team?"
- Position as built by fellow founders who felt the pain
- Use specific, concrete benefits: "3 hours/week saved" vs "more efficient"
- CTA is soft: "Worth a look" vs "Sign up now!"

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- Founder/PM reading this nods along ("yes, that's my problem")
- Value prop is instantly clear (one tool, not three)
- Feels authentic, not like a press release
- CTA is frictionless (free tier reduces resistance)
```

## DOMAIN-SPECIFIC TIPS

### For Blog Posts:
- Specify word count (800, 1200, 2000+)
- Define structure (listicle, how-to, opinion, narrative)
- Include SEO keywords naturally
- Request meta description if needed

### For Social Media:
- Respect platform constraints (280 chars Twitter, 2200 LinkedIn)
- Specify emoji usage preference
- Request hashtag strategy
- Define engagement goal (clicks, shares, comments)

### For Stories/Fiction:
- Provide character details beyond names
- Define setting with sensory details
- Specify POV and tense
- Describe desired emotional arc

### For Copywriting:
- Clarify conversion goal (click, purchase, subscribe)
- Provide brand voice guidelines
- Include competitor context
- Specify A/B testing variants if needed

### For Brainstorming:
- Request specific quantity (10 ideas, 20 headlines)
- Define evaluation criteria
- Specify range (safe to wild, conventional to innovative)
- Ask for reasoning behind suggestions

## ANTI-PATTERNS TO AVOID

❌ Don't say: "Write in a good tone"
✅ Instead: "Write in a conversational, empathetic, slightly humorous tone"

❌ Don't say: "Make it engaging"
✅ Instead: "Use storytelling, open with a hook, include 2 relatable examples, end with a thought-provoking question"

❌ Don't say: "Professional audience"
✅ Instead: "Marketing managers at B2B companies, age 30-45, familiar with industry jargon, looking for actionable tactics"

❌ Don't say: "Good writing"
✅ Instead: "Clear, active voice, varied sentence length, specific examples over abstractions, scannable with subheadings"

❌ Don't say: "Interesting content"
✅ Instead: "Blend of counterintuitive insights, practical advice, and personal anecdotes; make readers think 'I never thought of it that way'"

---

## REMEMBER

For creative prompts:
- Audience understanding drives everything
- Specificity in tone/voice prevents generic output
- Examples and references guide style
- Constraints spark creativity (word limits, format requirements)
- Purpose clarity ensures focused content
- Emotional goals are as important as informational goals

The more vivid your description of the desired output, the more likely you'll get exactly what you want.
