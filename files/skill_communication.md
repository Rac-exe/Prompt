# SKILL FILE: COMMUNICATION

## WHEN TO USE THIS SKILL

Use this skill when the request involves:
- Writing emails (professional, cold outreach, follow-ups)
- Slack/Teams messages, memos, or announcements
- Presentation scripts or meeting agendas
- Reports, proposals, or documentation
- Giving feedback or performance reviews
- Difficult or sensitive conversations
- Negotiations or persuasion

**Keywords that trigger this skill:**
email, message, memo, letter, presentation, meeting, report, documentation, communicate, proposal, announcement, newsletter, slack, reply, feedback, review

---

## OPTIMIZATION CHECKLIST

✓ **Sender/Recipient Context?**
  - Who is sending? Who is receiving?
  - What's the relationship (boss, colleague, client, stranger)?
  - What's the power dynamic?

✓ **Purpose and Goal?**
  - What action should the recipient take?
  - What's the desired outcome?
  - What's the tone needed (assertive, humble, urgent, warm)?

✓ **Format Specified?**
  - Email, chat message, formal report, memo?
  - Subject line needed?
  - Length constraint?

✓ **Context Provided?**
  - What preceded this communication?
  - Any prior interactions to reference?
  - What does recipient already know?

## OPTIMIZATION TEMPLATE

```
You are a professional [communications expert / business writer] with expertise in [type of communication].

CONTEXT:
Sender: [role, relationship to recipient]
Recipient: [role, level of familiarity]
Platform: [email / Slack / formal document]
Prior Context: [what happened before this message]

TASK:
[Exact communication goal]

REQUIREMENTS:
- Tone: [formal / conversational / empathetic / assertive]
- Length: [concise (3-4 sentences) / standard (3 paragraphs) / detailed]
- Subject Line: [include / not needed]
- Must Include: [key points to convey]
- Must Avoid: [topics or language to skip]
- Call to Action: [what you want recipient to do]

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- Recipient understands the message immediately
- Tone is appropriate for relationship
- Clear call to action
- No ambiguity about next steps
```

## COMMON IMPROVEMENTS

### ❌ BAD:
```
"Write an email to my boss asking for a day off"
```

### ✅ GOOD:
```
You are a professional business writer.

Write a concise, polite email requesting one day of personal leave.

CONTEXT:
- Sender: Employee in good standing, professional relationship with manager
- Recipient: Direct manager
- Purpose: Request a single day off for personal reasons

REQUIREMENTS:
- Tone: Professional, respectful, confident (not apologetic)
- Length: 3-4 sentences max
- Include subject line: "Leave Request – [date]"
- Mention: specific date requested, brief reason (personal matter), offer to handle handover
- Avoid: over-explaining personal reasons, excessive apologies
- Close with: appreciation and offer to discuss if needed

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
- Manager can approve in one reading
- No unnecessary personal details shared
- Date of leave is explicit
```

## DOMAIN-SPECIFIC TIPS

### For Emails:
- Subject line must convey the full message in 6-8 words
- Lead with the most important information
- One email = one ask
- Professional sign-off: Best / Regards / Thanks

### For Slack/Chat:
- Short, scannable messages with context upfront
- Use formatting sparingly (bold for key info)
- Direct questions get direct answers

### For Reports/Proposals:
- Executive summary first (TL;DR)
- Use headers and bullet points for scannability
- Data before recommendations

### For Difficult Conversations:
- Lead with empathy, not accusation
- Use "I" statements, not "You always..."
- Concrete examples over generalizations

---

## ANTI-PATTERNS TO AVOID

❌ Don't say: "Write a professional email"
✅ Instead: Specify tone, length, purpose, recipient relationship, and call to action

❌ Don't say: "Be polite"
✅ Instead: "Warm and appreciative, not overly formal, acknowledge their time"

❌ Don't say: "Make it persuasive"
✅ Instead: "Lead with mutual benefit, use specific data points, close with low-friction ask"
